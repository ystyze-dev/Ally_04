## Prototype testing

Objective

Evaluate the computational, physical, linguistic and relational behaviour of Ally before completing the final installation.

1. Linguistic testing


Recognize English heat vocabulary.


Recognize Spanish heat vocabulary.


Recognize mixed-language discourse.


Recognize repeated thermal references.


Recognize multiword expressions.


Recognize common verb inflections.


Distinguish unrelated speech from heat-related speech.


Evaluate metaphorical and implicit heat references.

2. Physical testing


Verify stillness.


Verify low-speed operation.


Verify medium-speed operation.


Verify high-speed operation.


Verify gradual acceleration.


Verify gradual deceleration.


Measure vibration.


Measure motor temperature.


Verify structural stability.


Verify safe motor shutdown.

3. Poetic testing


Detect prolonged silence.


Generate bilingual poetry.


Generate different thematic compositions.


Express the object's need for breath.


Display bilingual poetry for the subject to read, without voice playback.


Display the corresponding poetry.


Prevent self-generated speech from activating the fan.

4. Breath detection


Recognize gentle human exhalation.


Distinguish breath from speech.


Distinguish human breath from fan airflow.


Prevent accidental activation through environmental noise.


Acknowledge the reciprocal gesture.


Return to the listening state.

5. Reciprocal interaction


Subject speaks.


Object interprets heat-related language.


Object produces airflow.


Subject becomes silent.


Object gradually stops.


Object generates poetry.


Subject understands the poetic invitation.


Subject approaches the microphone.


Subject returns their breath.


Object acknowledges the gesture.


Interaction begins again.

6. Qualitative evaluation

Does the subject understand the relationship between language and airflow?

Does the object appear responsive to the subject's discourse?

Does silence communicate absence or expectation?

Does the poem successfully invite bodily participation?

Is the breath understood as a reciprocal gesture rather than a command?

Does the interaction establish a perceived relationship of mutual dependence?

Does the object transcend its conventional utilitarian function?

Testing documentation

For each experiment, record:

Date.

Prototype version.

Hardware configuration.

Environmental conditions.

Observed behaviour.

Unexpected behaviour.

Participant feedback.

Required modifications.

Conclusion

The success of Ally should be evaluated not only through technical performance but also through its capacity to establish a meaningful reciprocal relationship between the human subject and the object.
