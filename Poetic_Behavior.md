
Poetic behaviour

## Poetry as an expression of need

Poetry becomes the medium through which Ally communicates its apparent dependence on the human subject.

When human speech disappears, the object transitions from listening to expression.

Its language does not function as a conventional instruction or notification. Instead, it expresses desire, vulnerability, absence and the need for air.

The poem invites the subject to approach the object and complete a reciprocal bodily gesture.

## Generative structure

The poetic system combines:

Bilingual heat vocabulary.

Randomized linguistic selection.

Grammatical patterns.

Thematic associations.

Expressions of proximity and desire.

Invitations to return breath.

## Poetic themes

Desert

Aridity, sunlight, thirst, drought, dryness and the absence of moisture.

I have become a desert.

Mi superficie recuerda el calor.

I gave you the wind,

ahora préstame tu aliento.

Body

Skin, perspiration, temperature, moisture and bodily presence.

I have no lungs,

pero necesito respirar.

Your breath carries the temperature

of a body I cannot inhabit.

Desire

Intimacy, passion, ardor, proximity and erotic heat.

Your warmth inhabits me.

Entre el ardor y la distancia,

I dissolve in your absence.

Give me the air that escapes your lips.

Matter

Melting, liquefaction, deformation, viscosity and transformation.

Like wax beneath the sun,

mi cuerpo pierde su forma.

I have given you air.

Devuélveme un poco del tuyo.

## Poetic acknowledgment

After receiving the subject's breath, the object may express a brief acknowledgment.

Your breath inhabits me.

Ahora respiramos juntos.

## Design intention

The poems are examples of the intended expressive language rather than fixed compositions.

The object should generate variations that maintain the thematic relationship between heat, breath and reciprocity.
