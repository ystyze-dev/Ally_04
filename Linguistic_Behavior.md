
Linguistic behaviour

## Language as input and output

Ally operates through two complementary linguistic processes: interpretation and expression.

During interpretation, the object listens to the human subject.

During expression, the object generates language of its own.

## Receptive language

The object receives speech in English and Spanish and identifies references associated with heat.

These references may belong to different semantic dimensions:

Dimension	- Examples

Physical: Temperature, radiation, combustion
Environmental: Desert, drought, sunlight
Bodily:	Sweat, skin, fever
Emotional:	Anger, passion, affection
Erotic:	Desire, intimacy, ardor
Material:	Wax, melting, deformation
Atmospheric:	Humidity, vapor, condensation
Symbolic:	Transformation, renewal, memory

## Vocabulary structure

The bilingual vocabulary is organized into seven grammatical categories:

Nouns.

Verbs.

Adjectives.

Adverbs.

Prepositions.

Connectors.

Interjections and exclamations.

These categories allow the vocabulary to function as both a recognition database and a generative linguistic system.

## Expressive language

During silence, the object uses the vocabulary to construct bilingual poetic compositions.

The generated language expresses heat, desire, absence, vulnerability and the need for human breath.

## Linguistic hybridity

English and Spanish coexist within the poetic compositions.

The alternation between languages creates a hybrid linguistic structure rather than a conventional translation from one language into another.

The generated poetry may operate through association, fragmentation, repetition and semantic ambiguity.

## Current limitations

The current computational prototype recognizes vocabulary and thematic associations.

Full contextual interpretation of metaphor, implicit thermal references and extended discourse remains an area of development.
